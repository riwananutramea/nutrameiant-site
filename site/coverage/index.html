What we cover

Coverage is deliberately narrow. Nine markets, the authorities that regulate supplements in them, and the categories where registration rules actually differ. A wider list would be easier to publish and less useful to read.

Product categories

  • Vitamins and minerals

    Single and combination products, including the permitted upper limits that differ between markets.

  • Botanicals and herbals

    Where a plant sits between food supplement, traditional medicine and restricted substance, which is the most frequent cause of a rejected dossier.

  • Probiotics

    Strain level requirements, viability claims and the evidence each authority asks for.

  • Sports and weight management

    The category with the most enforcement activity, including banned ingredient lists and border rejections.

  • Infant and maternal

    Handled under separate rules in most of these markets, tracked separately here.

  • Medical nutrition

    Foods for special medical purposes, where the line to pharmaceutical registration matters most.

What tracked means

An authority is tracked when we read its published circulars, decisions and lists on a fixed schedule and record each change with its source and date. It does not mean we hold private information from that authority, and it does not make an entry legal advice. Registration decisions rest with the authority.
